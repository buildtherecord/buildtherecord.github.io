title: What to do when you didn't get the photo, video, or recording
short: The moment passed and you have nothing. You have more options than you think.

The noise happened and you did not have your phone out. The contractor made the
promise in a doorway. The representative said the thing and then the call ended.

This happens to everyone. It is not the end of that fact — but what you do in the
next hour matters a great deal.

## Do this first, right now

**Write it down today, with today's date.** What happened, when, who was there,
what was said as close to word-for-word as you can manage. Mark clearly which
parts are exact and which are your best recollection.

A dated note written the same day is real evidence. The same note written from
memory three months later is worth very little, and everyone in the process knows
the difference. **The clock on this is hours, not weeks.**

## Then, in rough order of strength

**1. Tell someone, in writing.** Text a friend or family member: *"The car just did
the shaking thing again on the highway, second time this week."* That creates a
dated record, held by someone other than you, made before you had any reason to
build a case. This is one of the most useful and least known moves available, and
it costs nothing.

**2. Ask them for their record.** Whoever you are dealing with very likely wrote
something down. Dealers log service calls. Insurers keep claim files and call
notes. Contractors send texts. **Request it before you argue with it.** Their
record of the same event is often better than the one you failed to capture — and
it is much harder for them to dispute.

**3. Look for the traces the event left behind.** You may not have the moment, but
the moment may have left a shadow: a service appointment booked the next morning,
a payment, a call in your phone log at the right time, a message you sent, a
warranty claim. None of these show what happened. Together they show that
*something* happened, when you say it did.

**4. Capture the next one.** If it is a recurring problem, it will recur. Set
yourself up now — phone within reach, know what you need in frame, know your
state's recording rule. The fact that you were ready and it happened again is
itself worth something.

**5. Recreate it — and label it, always.** Sometimes you can make the conditions
happen again and record that. This can be genuinely useful.

> **Never present a re-creation as the original.** Say plainly: *"This is a
> recording I made on [date], reproducing what happened on [earlier date]."*
>
> A labelled re-creation is honest evidence with a known limitation. An unlabelled
> one, once noticed, destroys your credibility on everything else you have said —
> including the parts that were true. It is not worth it. It is never worth it.

## When there is genuinely nothing

Say so.

Write "I did not capture this" in your own record. Do not describe it in the same
voice you use for things you can prove. A record that is honest about its own gaps
is stronger than one that quietly blurs the line, because a reader who finds one
overstatement starts doubting all of it.

The gap is survivable. Being caught papering over the gap is not.

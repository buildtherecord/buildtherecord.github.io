Build the Record

The record argues by existing.

Free, open methods and AI prompts for building a professional-grade case file — the dated, verified, quote-them-verbatim paper record that wins reviews, appeals, and disputes, or hands your lawyer a running start. Born inside a real self-represented case; everything specific removed, everything repeatable kept.

Who this is for

Representing yourself. The prompts walk an AI assistant through building your entire case file — inventory, timeline, ledgers, records requests, the adversarial review, the packet — while the decisions stay yours.
Preparing for a lawyer. The same file is the one every attorney wishes clients brought to the first meeting: complete, dated, honestly curated, with your own weak points flagged for counsel instead of discovered by the other side.

The doctrine, short version

One source of truth — every artifact generated from one catalogue, every number computed and asserted at build time. A document proves only what it shows. Quote them verbatim. Curate ruthlessly; never omit what would make you look dishonest. The counterparty-file principle: whoever decided against you assembled a file to do it — request it before you argue with it. Adversarial review before sending: have your own case attacked from the other side's perspectives, and fix what the hostile reading finds. State facts; the record argues by existing.

Contribute

Every method improves when someone runs it. Prompt fixes from real use, per-jurisdiction signpost files (dated and cited — see any repo's jurisdictions/), and anonymized outcomes are the contributions that compound. Real case documents are never accepted — the repos stay 100% synthetic. New domains are welcome: the fitness test is in each repo's story — disputes decided on paper, with a records right, a deadline ladder, and a lot of unrepresented people losing by default.

Not legal advice. These are methods for organizing evidence and process, not a substitute for a lawyer. Deadlines are real and jurisdiction-specific; the repos' own rule is that no deadline is ever taken from a template — everything is re-verified against primary sources at use. When the stakes are high, bring the record to counsel — that is half of what it is for.
